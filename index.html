ABOUT

A non-linear path into technology became one of my greatest strengths.

My background allows me to see technology from more than one angle: numbers, risk, business, customers and architecture. That combination shapes how I solve problems today.

I am a Solutions Architect with experience across banking technology, digital payments, cloud computing, integration design and enterprise solution architecture.

I started with a degree in Actuarial Science at KNUST, a discipline that sharpened how I think about risk, uncertainty, data and structured problem-solving. My early professional experience in risk and compliance deepened that perspective, but I became increasingly drawn to the systems and technology behind how organisations operate.

That curiosity led me into banking technology and eventually Solutions Architecture. Today, I work at the intersection of business needs and technical possibility, translating requirements into architectures, integration patterns, security considerations, process flows and implementation roadmaps that teams can actually build from.

My work has included payment infrastructure, digital banking channels, API-led integrations, telecom-to-bank connectivity, AI-enabled customer experience and mandate-management platforms. I particularly enjoy complex environments where multiple systems, stakeholders and constraints have to come together in one coherent solution.

I am also intentional about developing the architecture discipline itself. I hold the TOGAF® 10 Certified Practitioner credential and the AWS Certified Solutions Architect - Associate certification, and I continue to deepen my knowledge across cloud, enterprise architecture, integration and emerging technology.

Outside my day-to-day role, I mentor and speak about technology careers, personal branding, cloud computing and career transitions. I am especially interested in helping people who do not come from conventional computer-science backgrounds recognise that a technology career can be built from transferable skills, curiosity and deliberate positioning.

I want my career to sit at the intersection of technology, business value and impact: building useful systems, communicating complex ideas clearly, and creating room for other people to see what is possible for themselves.

FEATURED LINKEDIN WRITING PRODUCT · UX · INNOVATION

Innovation can be evolution, not just invention.

One idea I have explored is how small interaction patterns can move from one platform to another: YouTube's early double-tap interaction, for example, was later refined within TikTok-style experiences and became part of a wider digital design language.

The broader point is one I return to often: meaningful innovation does not always mean inventing something entirely new. It can mean observing user behaviour carefully, recognising what already works, and improving it in a way that feels natural enough to become a new standard.
